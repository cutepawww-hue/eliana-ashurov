Independent project

EEG Signal Processing & RC Filter Optimization

  • Python
  • LTspice
  • SciPy
  • NumPy
  • MNE-Python
  • Digital signal processing
  • Neural engineering

I designed and optimized a two-stage passive RC low-pass filter to study how electrical circuits affect neural signals in brain-computer interface (BCI) applications.

I modeled the circuit's transfer function, used SciPy to find resistor and capacitor values that meet set frequency-response targets, and checked the design in LTspice. I then applied its frequency response to real human EEG from the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset.

What I built

  • Circuit design. A two-stage RC low-pass filter in LTspice, with its transfer function derived by hand.
  • Numerical optimization. SciPy's Nelder–Mead algorithm tuned four components to keep at least 90% of amplitude at 10 Hz and pass at most 10% at 100 Hz in the mathematical model.
  • EEG processing. MNE-Python and NumPy for FFT-based filtering and inverse-FFT reconstruction of real recordings.
  • Signal analysis. Original and circuit-filtered EEG compared in the time and frequency domains.
  • Motor imagery (in progress). Finding left-hand and right-hand imagery segments to look at changes in mu (8–13 Hz) and beta (13–30 Hz) activity.

Next

Quantify motor-related EEG activity at the C3 and C4 electrodes, measure how well it survives the circuit filtering, and test whether extracted features can tell left-hand from right-hand imagery.

How does analog filter design change the neural information a brain-computer interface can use?

NYU Langone Health · Rusk Rehabilitation · Rizzo Lab

Eye-Tracking Accessibility for Neurological Rehabilitation

  • Biomedical engineering
  • Assistive technology
  • Eye tracking
  • Computational neuroscience
  • Python

I contributed to research on eye tracking as an accessible way to use a computer for people with neurological conditions, including Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis and progressive supranuclear palsy. The work focused on gaze-tracking accuracy and on adaptive calibration for atypical eye movements.

Contributions

  • Eye-movement analysis. Python analysis of saccades, fixations and gaze-position error.
  • Signal processing. Methods to separate natural fixation from rapid eye movements so gaze measurements are more reliable.
  • Adaptive calibration. Calibration approaches meant to improve tracking for users with neurological impairments.
  • Quantitative evaluation. Analysis of 144 eye-tracking trials involving supranuclear gaze palsy.

Why it matters

Standard eye trackers can struggle when a user's eyes move atypically, which can lock them out of gaze-controlled interfaces. Better calibration could help assistive communication, human-computer interaction and rehabilitation.

Can computational eye-movement analysis make gaze interfaces usable for people with movement disorders?
